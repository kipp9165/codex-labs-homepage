# Asynchronous Consulting OS Workflow

## Overview

Codex Labs operates on a fully asynchronous workflow. No real-time communication. No meetings. No calls. This document outlines how the consulting process works from intake through implementation.

---

## Phase 1: Intake

**Timeline:** Self-service, immediate

1. **You complete the intake form** on /intake.html
   - Share your current state
   - Share your desired state
   - Identify constraints
   - Define timeline
   - Surface known friction points
   - Upload any supporting docs (org charts, strategy docs, etc.)

2. **You submit**
   - Codex Labs receives intake
   - No automated response; we read thoroughly

---

## Phase 2: Review & Diagnosis

**Timeline:** 24–48 hours

1. **Codex Labs conducts deep read**
   - Analyze your current state
   - Map constraints and bottlenecks
   - Identify structural gaps
   - Diagnose root patterns
   - Prepare initial assessment

2. **You receive email with:**
   - Container recommendation (Clarity Sprint, Deep Dive, etc.)
   - Price range aligned to scope
   - Proposed engagement structure
   - Initial questions to clarify scope
   - Next steps

---

## Phase 3: Container Selection & Agreement

**Timeline:** Your response + 24 hours

1. **You choose your container**
   - Structural Clarity Sprint (7 days, $1,500–$3,000)
   - Ongoing Advisory (monthly, $2,000–$4,000/month)
   - Architecture Deep Dive (30 days, $5,000–$10,000)

2. **You receive Stripe invoice**
   - Payment upfront or split payment (50% upfront, 50% at midpoint)
   - No refunds
   - Payment constitutes acceptance of terms

3. **You pay; engagement begins**

---

## Phase 4: Engagement Setup

**Timeline:** 24 hours after payment

1. **Codex Labs sets up communication**
   - Shared document (Google Docs or Notion)
   - Single email channel
   - Access provided to you and your team (if applicable)

2. **Engagement framework is documented**
   - Scope of work clearly outlined
   - Deliverables listed
   - Timeline mapped
   - Communication protocol established

---

## Phase 5: Active Consultation

**Timeline:** Depends on container (7–30 days)

### Communication Structure

**Format:** Structured written messages in shared doc or email

**Cadence:** 24–48 hour response time (not continuous)

**One Question Per Round:** Each exchange focuses on one clear question or topic

**Context Always Included:** Every message includes background and rationale

### Typical Exchange

1. **You send message** with:
   - Context (what's happening)
   - Question (what you need clarity on)
   - Constraints (timeline, budget, resources)
   - Known variables (team size, stage, etc.)
   - Supporting docs (if needed)

2. **Codex Labs responds within 24–48 hours** with:
   - Analysis of situation
   - Answer to your question
   - Recommendation(s)
   - Next steps or follow-up questions
   - Documented in shared record

3. **You implement or respond**
   - You take time to process
   - You respond with implementation questions or next topic
   - No pressure for real-time back-and-forth

### No Calls

- If you try to schedule a call, the response is: "Let's handle this via written message. Send me your question with context."
- If you need faster turnaround, contact support for expedited response (additional fee may apply).

---

## Phase 6: Deliverables Creation

**Timeline:** Concurrent with consultation

Deliverables are documented as consultation progresses. Nothing is created at the end. Everything is written, reviewed, and finalized as you go.

### Standard Deliverables (per container)

**Clarity Sprint (7 days):**
- Clarity Map (visual diagnosis)
- Direction Statement (1-page intent)
- Immediate Actions (7-day plan)
- Risk Map (failure modes, dependencies)

**Deep Dive (30 days):**
- All Clarity Sprint deliverables PLUS:
- 30-Day Implementation Plan (sequenced actions)
- 6-Month Strategic Roadmap (milestones, continuity checkpoints)
- Momentum Protocol (weekly execution cadence)
- Org Structure Recommendations (if applicable)

**Ongoing Advisory (monthly):**
- Monthly Strategic Brief (what changed, what matters)
- Decision Guidance (on open questions)
- System Refinements (adjustments to plans)
- Continuity Checkpoints (keep momentum)

---

## Phase 7: Final Review & Handoff

**Timeline:** Day before engagement ends

1. **Final deliverables document is compiled**
   - All recommendations consolidated
   - All decisions documented
   - All rationale explained
   - Implementation roadmap clear

2. **You receive final summary**
   - What was discovered
   - What changed
   - What to focus on next
   - How to measure progress

3. **Access remains**
   - You keep access to all docs
   - You keep reference materials
   - You keep shared record forever

## Communication Protocol

### How to Write Effective Messages

**BAD:**
*"Hey, I'm stuck. Not sure what to do. Team is a mess. Help."*

**GOOD:**
*"Context: We've grown from 5 to 12 people in 2 months. Ownership is unclear. Two leads claim the same project. This is creating friction and delays. Question: How do we clarify ownership and decision authority without restructuring? Constraints: Everyone is already at capacity; we can't do a full org redesign. Timeline: Need clarity in 2 weeks."*

### Bad Patterns to Avoid

- Sending multiple questions at once (send one per message)
- Asking for clarification before providing context (context first, question second)
- Requesting calls or meetings (won't happen)
- Expecting responses outside 24–48 hour window (it won't happen faster)
- Sending follow-ups before receiving response (wait for answer)

### Good Practices

- Write clearly and specifically
- Provide context upfront
- Ask one question
- Include constraints
- Share relevant docs
- Wait for response before following up
- Implement recommendations; report back on progress

## What This Workflow Enables

1. **Thinking space.** We have time to analyze deeply, not respond reactively.
2. **Written record.** Everything is documented. No lost context.
3. **Your autonomy.** You decide when to engage, when to respond, when to implement.
4. **Scalability.** Async doesn't require real-time availability from either side.
5. **Quality.** Thoughtful guidance, not quick fixes.

---

## When Async Breaks Down

Async consulting is not for everyone. Red flags:

- You want calls or meetings
- You expect real-time response (you won't get it)
- You make decisions only in synchronous dialogue
- You need hand-holding or external validation
- You can't articulate your situation in writing
