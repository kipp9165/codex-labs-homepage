# Founder Boundary Scripts

## Overview

These are founder-grade scripts for maintaining boundaries around time, attention, and decision-making autonomy. Each script is realistic and can be adapted to your situation.

---

## Time Boundary Scripts

### When Someone Wants to "Quickly Sync"

**Script 1 (Direct):**

*"I don't do quick syncs. They're never quick, and I've noticed they create more questions than answers. If you have something specific, send me a message with context. I'll respond thoughtfully in the next 24 hours."*

**Script 2 (Collaborative):**

*"I've moved away from ad-hoc calls. It's helped me be more focused. If you need my input, send me a message with what you're deciding on + the options you're considering. I'll give you clear guidance."*

**Script 3 (Setting Up Process):**

*"Let's try this: send me a written message with your question and context. Give me 24 hours to respond. If that works better, we'll stick with it."*

---

### When Someone Wants "Just 15 Minutes"

**Script 1:**

*"Those 15-minute calls always turn into 45 minutes, and I end up context-switching for 2 hours after. Not worth it for either of us. Let's do this in writing instead."*

**Script 2:**

*"I've gone full async for decision-making. Send me what you're thinking about and what decision you need to make. I'll give you comprehensive feedback in writing."*

**Script 3:**

*"That's the problem — nothing is ever 'just 15 minutes.' Let's structure it differently: You send me context, I respond by [time]. Clear start and end."*

---

### When Someone Asks to Meet Daily or Weekly

**Script 1:**

*"Daily sync isn't sustainable for me — it kills deep work. Here's what we'll do instead: [weekly async check-in day, or weekly 30-min call]. Between those, async only."*

**Script 2:**

*"I'm moving to monthly tactical check-ins instead of weekly. More focus on what's blocking you, less on updates. When do you want to do our first one?"*

**Script 3:**

*"Weekly meetings feel like a lot. Let's try: You send me a status update in writing every Monday. I respond with feedback/guidance by Wednesday. See how that feels for two weeks."*

## Decision Boundary Scripts

### When Someone Seeks Your Approval for Everything

**Script 1:**

*"I need you to own this decision. Here's what I need to be comfortable: Give me the options + your recommendation. Tell me what you'd do. If you think it's right, do it."*

**Script 2:**

*"You don't need my permission. Bring me decisions when you're unsure, not every decision. What do you think we should do here?"*

**Script 3:**

*"Stop asking for approval. Make a decision. If it's wrong, we'll fix it. But I need you thinking independently, not running everything past me."*

---

### When Someone Wants to "Brainstorm" Every Problem

**Script 1:**

*"Brainstorming meetings aren't working for us. Here's what I need instead: Come to me with a problem + 2–3 options you've already thought through. Then we can talk."*

**Script 2:**

*"I notice we spend a lot of time brainstorming without deciding. Let's change this: You own the problem-solving. Come to me when you're stuck, not when you want ideas."*

**Script 3:**

*"Before we brainstorm, I need to know: What have you already tried? What's your thinking? That'll help me give better input."*

---

### When Someone Disagrees With Your Decision

**Script 1:**

*"I hear you. I've made my decision. I understand you see it differently. Here's how we'll move forward: [decision]. Let's talk again in 2 weeks and see what happened."*

**Script 2:**

*"I appreciate the push-back. But this is my call to make. I'm going with [decision]. If it doesn't work, we'll adjust. If you're uncomfortable, let's talk about your concerns."*

**Script 3:**

*"I get that you disagree. I've thought about this. This is what I'm doing. I need your buy-in on execution, not agreement with the decision."*

## Attention Boundary Scripts

### When Someone Texts You at 2 AM With a Problem

**Script 1 (Next morning):**

*"Got your message. I don't check messages after 10 PM. If it's truly urgent (life/death/legal), call me. Otherwise, I respond during working hours. What's the situation?"*

**Script 2:**

*"I'm not checking messages outside 8 AM–6 PM. If this is urgent, call. If not, send it via email and I'll get to it tomorrow."*

**Script 3:**

*"I appreciate you thinking of me, but I'm not available at that hour. Send it via email and I'll respond first thing tomorrow. Is it urgent?"*

---

### When Someone Sends Multiple Messages Back-to-Back

**Script 1:**

*"I notice you're sending a lot of quick messages. It fragments my attention. Could you bundle them into one message with all the context? That helps me give better answers."*

**Script 2:**

*"Give me one comprehensive message, not five rapid-fire questions. I'll respond more thoroughly if I have the full picture."*

**Script 3:**

*"I can't context-switch between 10 small questions. Write them all down. Send one message. I'll answer all of them at once."*

---

### When Someone Wants You to Attend Every Meeting

**Script 1:**

*"I can't be in every meeting. Here's what I need to be in: [decision-critical meetings, weekly all-hands, monthly planning]. Everything else: give me notes or a recording."*

**Script 2:**

*"You don't need me in that meeting. You're capable of deciding. Tell me what you decided and why. If I have input, I'll give it."*

**Script 3:**

*"Stop inviting me to meetings about my attendance. Who needs to be there? Make a list. Get my input on that. Otherwise, decide and move forward."*

## Workload Boundary Scripts

### When Your Team Is Asking Too Much

**Script 1:**

*"I'm at capacity. I can't take on [project/request]. Here's what I can do instead: [alternative]. Can one of you own the rest?"*

**Script 2:**

*"I have three competing priorities right now. I can focus on [#1]. The other two need to wait or someone else needs to lead them. Which would you prefer?"*

**Script 3:**

*"That sounds important, but it's not in my priority list right now. I need you to solve this one. If you're stuck, let me know the specific question."*

---

### When Someone Adds Tasks to Your List Without Asking

**Script 1:**

*"Please don't add things to my list without asking. My time is constrained. Check with me first. I'll let you know if I can take it."*

**Script 2:**

*"I've said yes to too much. Going forward, if you need something from me, ask me first. I may say no."*

**Script 3:**

*"I need you to manage my bandwidth better. Before you ask me to do something, ask yourself: Is this mission-critical? Is it your job or mine? Can it wait?"*

## Compensation/Equity Boundary Scripts

### When Someone Asks You to "Work for Equity"

**Script 1:**

*"I don't do equity-only arrangements for work. Equity is meaningful only if the company succeeds. Cash pays my rent now. I'd need [salary + equity] to do this."*

**Script 2:**

*"Equity is not salary. If you're asking me to work, we need to agree on cash compensation. Equity is secondary. What are you offering on the cash side?"*

**Script 3:**

*"That equity might be valuable someday. But I need to be paid now. Let's talk real salary, and the equity is on top. What can you offer?"*

---

### When Someone Expects You to Work Uncompensated

**Script 1:**

*"I charge for my time. I'm happy to help, but there's a fee. What's your budget? What specific problem are we solving?"*

**Script 2:**

*"Free advice often leads to misalignment. If you want real guidance, I charge for it. Here's the investment. Here's what you get."*

**Script 3:**

*"I can give you 30 minutes of free thinking. Beyond that, it's a consulting engagement. Are you interested?"*

## Energy/Focus Boundary Scripts

### When Someone Wants You to Be Available 24/7

**Script 1:**

*"I'm available [specific hours]. Outside those hours, I'm focused on family/rest/deep work. If it's an emergency, call my emergency line. Otherwise, it waits."*

**Script 2:**

*"I protect my focus time. I'm not available evenings/weekends. Plan accordingly. If you need something from me, plan for it during business hours."*

**Script 3:**

*"I'm not checking Slack after 6 PM or before 8 AM. My brain needs to be off to be effective during the day. Message me anytime, but I'll respond the next business day."*

---

### When You're Burned Out and Need to Say No

**Script 1:**

*"I'm at a breaking point. I need to reduce my workload. I'm stepping back from [X, Y, Z]. This is non-negotiable. Here's how we're managing it: [transition plan]."*

**Script 2:**

*"I'm realizing I've overcommitted. I need to renegotiate what I'm responsible for. Let's talk about what stays and what goes."*

**Script 3:**

*"I'm tired. I need to focus on [core thing]. Everything else is secondary or delegated. I know this affects you. Let's figure out how to adjust."*

## Communication Style Boundary Scripts

### When Someone is Being Disrespectful in Tone

**Script 1:**

*"I notice your tone lately has been [curt/aggressive/dismissive]. I'm open to feedback, but I need respect. Can we reset?"*

**Script 2:**

*"I'm not going to engage with that tone. Let's talk about this differently, or let's not talk about it right now."*

**Script 3:**

*"I hear you're frustrated. I get it. But I'm not going to accept that language. Let's cool off and come back to this tomorrow."*

---

### When Someone is Venting and You Don't Have Space for It

**Script 1:**

*"I hear you're frustrated. I don't have space to process this right now. Here's what I can do: [listen for 10 min, or connect you with someone, or talk tomorrow]."*

**Script 2:**

*"I care about you, but I'm at capacity emotionally right now. This conversation needs someone with more space. Can we do this [time/with someone else]?"*

**Script 3:**

*"I want to be present for this, but I can't be right now. Let's schedule time when I can give you my full attention."*

## How to Use These Scripts

1. **Adapt them to your voice.** These are templates, not exact words.
2. **Say them early.** Don't wait until you're resentful.
3. **Be consistent.** If you don't enforce the boundary, it doesn't stick.
4. **Explain the why.** People respect boundaries more when they understand the reasoning.
5. **Offer an alternative.** Don't just say no; offer a yes to something else.

## Common Boundary Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Being too nice.**
- "I'm not great with availability, sorry 😬" (weak)
- vs. "I work [hours]. Outside that, I'm not available." (clear)

**Mistake 2: Explaining too much.**
- "I could do a call, but I've been realizing that calls aren't my best way to think, and I find they interrupt my flow, so..." (rambling)
- vs. "I work async. Send me a message." (clear)

**Mistake 3: Not following through.**
- Setting a boundary on calls, then taking a call anyway (destroys credibility)
- Don't set boundaries you won't keep.

**Mistake 4: Apologizing for boundaries.**
- "I'm so sorry, but I can't..." (sounds optional)
- vs. "I don't do that." (sounds non-negotiable)

---

## Remember

Boundaries aren't selfish. They're:

- **Respect for yourself.** You have limited time and energy.
- **Clarity for others.** People know where you stand.
- **Better for the work.** You're more effective when you're focused and rested.
- **Professional.** Clear boundaries create professional relationships.
